# Rational Recovery on a Common Ruler

When do the two coordinates of a rational plane curve determine its parameter by rational operations? For the family below, the parametrization is birational onto its image for every choice of the two pole orders and the pole location, with one explicit degree-two exception.

## The classification

Fix a real ruler $K>1$, integers $n,m\ge2$, and an indeterminate $x$. Take the readings

$$
F_n(x)=\frac{x-1}{x^n},\qquad G_{m,K}(x)=\frac{(x-1)^2}{(x-K)^m}.
$$

**Theorem.** The two readings together generate the entire rational function field $\mathbb R(x)$ for every choice of $n,m,K$ **except** $n=2,\ m=4,\ K=2$, where the extension has degree two. Thus, outside the exception, $x$ can be expressed as a rational function of the two readings. This is a statement about recovery at a generic point. An individual formula can have poles or give $0/0$ at particular data. [Formal specification](Challenge.lean) · [Classification theorem](Classification.lean)

At the exception, write $F=F_2$. Then $G_{4,2}=F^2/(1-4F)^2$, so the second measurement adds no information. The involution $x\mapsto x/(x-1)$ preserves both readings. For example, $x=3/2$ and $x=3$ both give $(F,G)=(2/9,4)$. Only $3/2$ belongs to the real interval $(1,2)$. This illustrates the difference between the degree of a rational parametrization and uniqueness after imposing an interval. [Proof of the exceptional degree](CommonRuler/Resonance.lean)

### Why there is only one exception

Write $d$ for the degree of $\mathbb R(x)$ over the field generated by both readings. The degree of every nonconstant rational function in that field is divisible by $d$. The individual readings have degrees $n$ and $m$, respectively. Hence $d$ divides both. This already settles pairs of coprime orders, including $(7,3)$. Common factors require more information. [Degree calculation](CommonRuler/FieldDegree.lean)

Set $H=F_n^2/G_{m,K}=(x-K)^m/x^{2n}$. If $m<2n$, the reduced degree of $H^n/F_n$ is $(2n-1)n+1$, coprime to $n$. If $m>2n$, the reduced degree of $H^n F_n$ is $mn+1$, coprime to $m$. Either gives $d=1$. This part holds over any coefficient field with $K\ne0,1$. [Nonresonant proof](CommonRuler/FunctionField.lean)

At $m=2n$, the leading pole can instead be canceled. For $K\ne n$, the numerator of $H-K^{2n}F_n^2$ has a simple zero at $x=0$ before cancellation. For $K=n>2$, the numerator of $(K^nF_n-(K-1))/H$ has a simple zero at $x=K$ before cancellation. Both give an element of degree $2n-1$, coprime to $n$. The sole remaining parameters give the explicit involution above. [Resonant proof](CommonRuler/Resonance.lean)

This is a question in rational curve parametrization. Sendra and Winkler study the tracing index, which measures how often a parametrization covers its image generically. Alonso, Gutiérrez and Recio give a general algebraic approach to rational function decomposition and testing birationality. Here the two functions are specified in advance. The result is a single classification across unbounded exponents and a continuously varying pole location. [Sendra and Winkler (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-8396(01)00069-3) · [Alonso, Gutiérrez and Recio (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsco.1995.1030)

## Why a common ruler?

In the motivating PDT application, one formula fixes a product $K=ab$, the common ruler. Two further formulas read its split through nonzero multiples of $F_7(b)$ and $G_{3,K}(b)$. This gives the member $(n,m)=(7,3)$ of the family. The application, including the exponent-15 example, is documented separately. [Gauge calculation and Lean proofs](GAUGE_APPLICATION.md) · [Earlier arithmetic registration](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-19-000013&version=1)

## Checking the theorem

The single comparator target is `PDTGauge.Result.joint_degree_classification`. The challenge gives its statement and the definitions of the two functions. The solution uses the degree calculation and the two cases in the proof above. [Comparator](comparator.json) · [Challenge](Challenge.lean) · [Solution](Classification.lean)

Using the pinned Lean toolchain, run

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
PATH="$(lean --print-prefix)/bin:$PATH" lake comparator
```

On macOS the local comparator uses `--inadvisably-no-sandbox`. The optional exact cross-check uses SymPy 1.14. Running `python3 check_function_field.py` computes 341 bivariate polynomial gcds for the degree classification and checks the gauge inverse at 25 rational inputs.
